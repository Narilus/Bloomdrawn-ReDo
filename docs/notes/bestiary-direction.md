# Bestiary direction: owner's review of the M6.5 draft

Status: **decided by the owner, 31 Oct** (written up with Claude in the planning chat). It revises `bestiary.md` as follows:

- **Band 1** stays as drafted.
- **Band 5** stays as drafted.
- **Bands 2, 3 and 4** are replaced by the rosters below. Band 3 keeps the draft's expedition concepts but redraws them.
- **The Sapper Crew is rejected**, along with the idea of Shield-ignoring damage.

Items marked **decided** are settled. Items marked **recommendation** are Claude's call and the owner's to overturn. Names, HP and damage are placeholders.

This note explains the reasons behind each rule, because that's where earlier builds went wrong. When a case isn't covered, work out which reason applies and follow it. If two reasons conflict, ask rather than choose silently.

## 1. Why the draft is being redone

Almost every draft enemy is *an ordinary person, animal or object with a few petals added*: a villager in an apron, an ox, a ram, a miller, a bell. The Bloom sits on top of their old lives as decoration. Two problems follow:

- **Depth doesn't look deeper.** A Depth 35 enemy is no further into the Bloom than a Depth 12 one; only the setting changes.
- **It's not the game's pull.** The owner wants monstrous art direction: really busy and wonderful designs at the higher depths. Pillar 6 asks that the beauty and the horror come from the same source. An ox with petals has neither.

Farm and village settings are out. Band 3's expedition concepts are worth keeping, because the people are already remade. Band 5's root-heart stays as drafted.

## 2. Mechanics decisions

### 2.1 No enemy damage ignores Shield (decided; add to DESIGN.md as a rule)

Shield is the party's only universal defence, and there is no healer kit. Unblockable damage scaled to Depth 40+ numbers would be a random run-ender with no answer. This rule applies to every enemy, elite and boss, now and later, including Trial bosses. The rejected Sapper Crew tested Shield-only defence; that test is dropped, not reworked.

### 2.2 Burst, then withers (decided: keep, with these four conditions)

The Garden Husk's self-destroying Burst stays, because it rewards reading intents. The player chooses to kill it first, Shield up, or take the hit for tempo. To keep it a choice and not a tax:

1. **It counts down visibly.** Its Ripen turn shows the Burst coming a turn ahead.
2. **Shield blocks it**, as 2.1 requires.
3. **It stays under the single-hit ceiling** the lab already uses: a single hit stays near half the party's HP or less.
4. **Killing it before it Bursts cancels the Burst.** There is no death explosion: dying must never punish the player for answering it.

### 2.3 Answers to the draft's questions (decided)

- **Q1:** a depth draws from its own band and the one before.
- **Q2:** each Boss's Full Bloom is a different second bar, as the Abbess's is.
- **Q3:** yes, an enemy may make you draw 1 fewer next turn, shown on its intent. The thin-hand test needs it.
- **Q4:** no (2.1).
- **Q5:** yes, with the conditions in 2.2.
- **Q6:** one band at a time, and the owner picks each band's art sheet before the next band starts.

## 3. Art direction

### 3.1 How far the Bloom has gone, rising with depth (decided)

Each band is one rung further into the Bloom:

| Band | Depths | Rung | What it means for a design |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–10 | **Taken** | A recognisable creature or person with Bloom growths (today's enemies) |
| 2 | 11–20 | **Hybrid** | Two or three animals fused into one body, with the joins visible |
| 3 | 21–30 | **Remade** | People and their gear grown into single bodies |
| 4 | 31–40 | **Made** | The Bloom's own creatures, with no single host, built from flower and body parts |
| 5 | 41–50 | **The source** | The root-heart and its templates (as drafted) |

**Why:** depth should *look* deeper. The player should be able to tell roughly how deep they are from the enemies alone. This ladder also limits the art: a band 2 design should not look like a band 4 one.

### 3.2 Design principles (decided)

1. **Busy inside, clear outside.** Every design has one strong silhouette that reads at battle-sprite size, with dense detail inside it. The detail rises with depth: band 2 is moderate, and band 4 is the busiest in the game.
   - **Why:** battle sprites are side-view HD pixel art seen at a distance. Busy detail without a clear shape turns into noise. A clear shape full of detail reads as wondrous.
2. **Show the seams.** Wherever bodies or materials join, draw the join: sutures, petal collars, root-wrapped joints, skin grown over brass or canvas.
   - **Why:** the join is where the horror and the wonder are. A seam says "this was made", which is the Bloom's whole story.
3. **One beautiful element per monster:** for example a perfect peony, lace-edged wings, glowing pollen, or a gold-veined petal.
   - **Why:** pillar 6. Without a beautiful element a design is just gore, and Flesh's never-list already bans gore without the flower.
4. **The glow rises with depth.** Deeper monsters carry more of their own pink and lilac light from flesh, petals, pods and pollen.
   - **Why:** the Bloom is the light source in every scene. Deeper bands should look brighter and stranger, not darker and murkier.
5. **No mundane jobs as identity.** No aprons, trade tools as the main idea, or plain livestock. A human trace is allowed as a *remnant*: a hand, a boot, a sleeve, a face half-closed in petals.
   - **Why:** the owner rejected "men in aprons, an ox, a bell". A remnant reminds the player what was taken. A job makes the enemy a costume.
6. **Run enemies stay out of the Domain looks:** cult hoods and robes, kitsune or mythic-creature sources, surgery as costume.
   - **Why:** the four Trial bosses (M6.7) carry those themes. Band 3's surgical grafts are allowed, because they are what was *done to* the crew (section 4.2), not a costume.
7. **Watch the hands.** Many-armed designs end in blades, tendrils, joints or tools, not drawn fingers. Where a hand must show, keep it one hand, simple and clear.
   - **Why:** generated art gets hands wrong. Kibane's options were rejected for bad hands.

### 3.3 Sizes (recommendation)

Busy designs need pixels. Use the existing sizes generously:

- band 2 normals: normal or large;
- band 3–4 normals: large;
- elites: large;
- bosses: huge.

Summons stay small. The layout rule still holds: independently targetable enemies never overlap their bodies, target areas, HP or intent labels.

### 3.4 Bloom-stage overlays (recommendation)

DESIGN.md gives enemies overlays for the later clock stages. For the busy deep-band designs, an overlay should add **glow and open petals** to the existing anatomy rather than grow new body parts. Each overlay is then a variant of one drawing rather than a new drawing.

#### 4.2.1 The Expedition Leader: look

**Decided by the owner:** he is mostly human, and he has **embraced the Bloom in a monstrous, humanoid way**.

- **Figure:** a tall, gaunt man in his fifties, with grey swept-back hair and a lined face. He stands upright, shoulders back, the saw held low.
- **Clothes:** a long oxblood survey coat, open to the waist. Wire spectacles with one cracked lens.
- **The Bloom:** his chest is opened down the breastbone into a great flesh flower. His ribs are splayed outward as pale petals, with a wet red heart at the centre. This is the one place in the band where the horror centre shows openly; DESIGN.md says centres are used sparingly, and this is where it is spent.
- **Hands and tools:** fine pale root threads hang from the fingertips of his free hand to the ground, each ending in a curved steel surgical needle. The other hand holds a brass-handled bone saw. A leather surgical roll at his hip has pale shoots growing through the tools.
- **Face:** human, with a calm expression and a faint closed-lip smile. Small blush petals grow around both eyes.
- **Colours:** oxblood, brass, ivory bone, raw pink, arterial red.
- **Silhouette:** a man's shape, drawn at the huge size so he matches other bosses on stage. The splayed rib-petals widen his chest, and the coat's long hem widens his base.
- *Optional second-bar variant:* the same figure, with the chest-flower open wider and the rib-petals reaching his shoulders.

*Design note:* his calm is what makes him frightening, and his crew are his work. Neither can be drawn into his sprite; both come through his face and pose and the band around him.

## 5. New abilities

Everything is shown on the intent before the player commits; nothing is hidden.

### 5.1 Cocoon: one turn of immunity in a cycle (decided, owner, 31 Oct)

The owner accepts **one turn** of immunity, because bosses already have one when they Rouse. He does not accept a fight-long lockout, or anything that ruins Rapture's gameplay. So:

- **One turn in four, never alternating.** The Scout's cycle is Cocoon, Unfurl, then two ordinary actions. At most one of every four player turns faces a cocooned Scout. Never put Cocoon twice in a cycle or make the cycle shorter.
- **Reuse the existing immunity.** Use the Hollow Abbess's Rouse immunity rule rather than writing a new one. It lasts until the Scout's next action (Unfurl).
- **Status ticks still deal damage, and statuses can still be applied.** The test is *timing*: build up while it's closed, burst when it opens. Statuses are part of building up, and blocking them would hard-counter Flesh. If the Abbess's immunity blocks status ticks today, ask the owner before changing either rule.
- **Unfurl rewards the wait.** The Scout is Vulnerable until its next action, so the turn after the Cocoon is the best turn to burst. That also pays Rapture back for the volley it lost.
- **Rapture only loses one volley.** Volleys don't spend Tentacles, so Rapture keeps building through the closed turn and loses at most that turn's end-of-turn volley damage. If volleys today aim at an immune enemy when another target exists, make them skip immune enemies as a general rule, not a special case for the Scout.
- **Check in the lab** that a Rapture party clears the Scout within the elite turn target. If it doesn't, report it rather than special-casing Rapture.

## 6. What not to infer

- **Don't** give any enemy damage that ignores Shield, ever (2.1). This includes Trial bosses and "just one big boss move".
- **Don't** add a death explosion or on-death damage to the Husk or anything else (2.2).
- **Don't** reintroduce farm, village or job-based enemies as a design's identity (3.2.5). A remnant is fine; a costume is not.
- **Don't** design deeper enemies that are *less* transformed than shallower ones. Check every design against the ladder (3.1).
- **Don't** explain what the Expedition Leader did, in lore, flavour or tooltips (4.2). Show it in the crew.
- **Don't** make the Expedition Leader into another monstrosity. He must still read as a man (4.2.1).
- **Don't** let any band-3 crew design lose its human remnant; it's the evidence of what he did.
- **Don't** add hidden or unpreviewed enemy effects (DESIGN.md pillar 4), and never let an enemy crit (`m6-scaling.md`).
- **Don't** give the Scout, or any future enemy, immunity for more than one turn in a cycle, or a shorter cycle (5.1). The owner allows one turn of immunity, like a Rouse, never a lockout.
- **Don't** draw all of the bands' art up front. One band at a time, and the owner picks each sheet first (2.3).
